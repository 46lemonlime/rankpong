# RankPong — Architectural Decision Records (ADRs)

This document records the foundational product and engineering decisions agreed upon for RankPong. These records serve as an authoritative source of truth to maintain architectural discipline and guide future development.

---

## 1. Accepted Architectural Decisions

### ADR-01: Public Multi-Club Platform
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: RankPong needs to serve a broad audience, from casual workplace groups to competitive regional clubs.
- **Decision**: RankPong operates as a public multi-tenant platform where any registered user can discover clubs, create new clubs, or request membership.
- **Consequences**: User profiles and authentication are global across the platform, while club data boundaries are segregated at the row level.

---

### ADR-02: Private Clubs by Default
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Clubs want autonomy and privacy for internal matches, conversations, and ladders.
- **Decision**: Clubs are private by default. Public visitors can view basic discovery metadata (name, slug, logo, member count), but internal rosters, match records, and discussions are restricted strictly to active members.
- **Consequences**: Row Level Security (RLS) policies must verify active membership before granting read or write access to club-scoped tables.

---

### ADR-03: Multi-Club Membership
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Players frequently play in multiple settings (e.g. workplace lunch breaks, local community club on weekends).
- **Decision**: A single user account can join and participate in multiple clubs simultaneously.
- **Consequences**: Membership is modeled through a many-to-many relationship (`club_members`) with separate status and role tracking per club.

---

### ADR-04: Tripartite Club Role Hierarchy (Owner, Admin, Member)
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Clubs need governance without bureaucratic complexity.
- **Decision**: Standardize on three club-specific roles:
  - **OWNER**: Complete authority, settings control, admin assignment, ownership transfer, club deletion.
  - **ADMIN**: Member management, match dispute resolution, competition administration. Cannot transfer ownership.
  - **MEMBER**: Private content access, match recording, competition participation, leaderboard viewing.
- **Consequences**: Permission checks can be evaluated cleanly in RLS policies and server actions based on this fixed enum.

---

### ADR-05: Decentralized Club-Controlled Membership
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Clubs need to prevent unauthorized players from joining their private ladder.
- **Decision**: Membership is strictly controlled by each club through either:
  1. A join request submitted by a user and approved by a club Owner or Admin.
  2. A direct invitation token generated by a club Owner or Admin.
- **Consequences**: Membership state machine includes `pending`, `active`, `rejected`, `banned`, and `left`.

---

### ADR-06: Global Universal ELO Independent of Clubs
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Players want a single credible measure of skill that follows them regardless of which club they join or leave.
- **Decision**: Every player has a single global ELO rating starting at 1000. It is universal across RankPong and independent of club membership or competition standings.
- **Consequences**: ELO is calculated and stored on the global `profiles` record, and historical changes are recorded in `rating_history`.

---

### ADR-07: Confirmed Ranked Matches Exclusively Update ELO
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Rating inflation, fraudulent entries, and mistakes can undermine competitive integrity.
- **Decision**: Only confirmed ranked matches produce rating changes. A match must be in `status = 'confirmed'` via opponent mutual verification (or admin dispute resolution) before ELO deltas are committed.
- **Consequences**: Match creation does not immediately alter ratings; rating calculation occurs as a secondary atomic step upon confirmation.

---

### ADR-08: Friendly Matches Do Not Affect Global ELO
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Players often play casual warm-up games, test new paddles, or teach beginners without wanting to risk their competitive rating.
- **Decision**: Matches designated as `category = 'friendly'` are recorded in club history and player stats but never alter global ELO ratings.
- **Consequences**: Match submission forms must allow players to toggle between Ranked and Friendly.

---

### ADR-09: Competitions Have Independent Standings
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Leagues and tournaments follow their own points systems (e.g. 3 points for win, 1 for draw, ladder differentials) which differ from pure ELO.
- **Decision**: Competitions calculate standings (points, win/loss, ladder rank) completely independently of global ELO. A player might rank first in a tournament while having a moderate ELO.
- **Consequences**: Competition standings are stored or calculated in competition-specific tables (`competition_standings`) without mutating `profiles.elo_rating`.

---

### ADR-10: Optional ELO Impact for Competition Matches
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Some tournaments are serious sanctioned events that should impact ratings, while others are fun handicap tournaments.
- **Decision**: Club administrators can configure whether matches within a given competition also count as official ranked matches that update global ELO.
- **Consequences**: The competition schema includes an `affects_global_elo` boolean flag that controls whether match confirmation triggers the ELO engine.

---

### ADR-11: Supabase as Primary Backend and Database
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Need a scalable PostgreSQL database with enterprise-grade auth, row-level security, and SSR cookie integration.
- **Decision**: Use Supabase for PostgreSQL, Supabase Auth, Row Level Security, and `@supabase/ssr` session management, coupled with Resend for transactional email.
- **Consequences**: No custom auth backend is built; security is anchored in database-level RLS policies.

---

### ADR-12: TypeScript Strict Mode Throughout
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Prevent runtime type errors and maintain code consistency across domain slices.
- **Decision**: TypeScript strict mode is enforced throughout the entire codebase (`strict: true` in `tsconfig.json`). Usage of `any` is strictly avoided.
- **Consequences**: Database schemas must be synchronized to TypeScript types via the Supabase CLI (`types/supabase.ts`).

---

### ADR-13: MVP Prioritisation (Auth, Clubs, Memberships, Matches)
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Overambitious initial scopes delay releases and introduce unnecessary complexity.
- **Decision**: The MVP focuses strictly on user authentication, club creation, membership management, match recording with mutual confirmation, and global ELO calculations. Competitions (leagues/tournaments) and doubles matches are deferred to subsequent phases.
- **Consequences**: Initial development effort remains targeted and testable.

---

### ADR-14: Future Features Must Not Unnecessarily Complicate MVP
- **Status**: Accepted
- **Context**: Designing for every future edge case leads to premature abstraction and difficult maintenance.
- **Decision**: The core domain model accounts for future expansion (e.g. doubles, tournaments, seasons), but initial code implementations must remain direct, lean, and free of speculative abstractions.
- **Consequences**: Database schemas have foreign keys and enums prepared for future entities, but frontend and server action code does not implement speculative interfaces.

---

## 2. Open Decisions (Pending Future Evaluation)

The following architectural topics remain open and will be evaluated during their respective phases:

1. **Doubles Rating Algorithm**:
   - *Options*: Average team ELO, dedicated separate doubles rating, or individual delta attribution.
   - *Target Phase*: Phase 6 (Expansion).
2. **Provisional ELO Period Duration**:
   - *Options*: Fixed 20 matches with $K=32$, or dynamic Glicko-style rating deviation (RD).
   - *Target Phase*: Phase 4 (Rankings).
3. **Automated Match Auto-Confirmation Window**:
   - *Options*: Auto-confirm after 72 hours of opponent inactivity, or require explicit admin intervention.
   - *Target Phase*: Phase 3 (Matches).
4. **Push Notification Provider**:
   - *Options*: Web Push API via service workers, or third-party provider (OneSignal / Expo).
   - *Target Phase*: Phase 6 (Expansion).
